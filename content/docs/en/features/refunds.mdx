## How to use it

Tenant operators open Refunds, inspect the order and available amounts, preview the selected quantities/value, then initiate through the authorized flow. Follow provider completion or settle/withdraw manual-pending refunds with evidence. Vendors inspect their own refund share.

Vendor staff can inspect refund information. Authorized administrators can close the order refund once all successful refunds for that order cover its full total. A partial refund keeps the order open.

In **Account → Payments**, open **Refunds** to see the amount, source purchase, destination, and progress. An eligible cancellation refund can appear while it is pending. Its credit note is available once issued. If a failed refund has a replacement, the history identifies the replacement and shows its own status. The store confirms any method-specific timing.

## Restrictions and decisions

With finance access, open **Marketplace Operations** and select **Refund reconciliation** for a dispatched refund whose result is still unclear. Request a check or submit a stored event ID or provider reference. The original amount remains reserved while the outcome is unknown. If the provider conclusively verifies that an eligible leg never executed, authorized refund staff can request a replacement for that leg. Successful legs stay settled.

In a local development preview, guarded test tooling can confirm or decline a previously dispatched test-payment refund. Confirmation updates the normal refund history and balances. A decline releases only that refund’s reservation; other pending refunds remain reserved. These are simulated results, not money returned by a live provider.

For new checkouts, the refund ceiling uses what was actually paid after coupons and redeemed loyalty, including the frozen tax. Wallet and gift cards are forms of payment; they do not reduce this ceiling again. Example: two items worth 100 EGP, with 20 EGP paid using loyalty, leave 80 EGP of money. Refunding one unit can return at most 40 EGP of money, with any loyalty restoration handled separately.

A monetary adjustment is different from recording returned units. The API supports an explicit zero quantity for a money-only adjustment; it records no returned units and does not restore unit-based loyalty. Staff should use the approved return workflow to record physical returns. Historical purchases retain their original compatibility rules; inconsistent financial evidence requires review rather than a guessed refund.

The ceiling is the smaller of the order’s remaining refundable value and shared payment headroom. Pending refunds reserve value. Duplicate line IDs are invalid, quantities cap value, and a sibling vendor order’s funds cannot cover an over-refund. Vendor access is read-only.

## Data flow and outcome

Eligible order lines → preview allocation → refund command → online confirmation or manual settlement → refund ledger, payment totals and credit note. Partial refunds across siblings do not mark the whole shared payment fully refunded.

## Follow the vendor balance after success

In the pre-release financial correction, a successful original-line refund reduces the vendor balance by its supported gross amount, including the recorded refund tax. This remains true at a zero commission rate. The related platform fee reversal is separate, and cumulative full refunds consume the remaining reversible fee rather than leaving a rounding cent behind.

Creating a request or waiting for a provider does not prove the refund succeeded. Statement selection uses actual success/settlement evidence. A financial refund with no returned units does not prove goods came back, were restocked or were delivered. Missing original seller ownership or success timing requires review. Repairing a delayed fee reversal does not send the customer another refund or rewrite the original purchase.

## When something goes wrong

Check workspace, permission, record state and field errors first. If an action times out, inspect the record and its history before retrying. Escalate with the screen, record identifier and time; omit passwords and payment secrets.

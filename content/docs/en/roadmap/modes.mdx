## Two modes on one commerce foundation

Store mode sells tenant-operated offers. Marketplace mode adds approved third-party sellers and can retain tenant-operated offers alongside them. Enabling Marketplace does not require a new tenant, new customer base or replacement storefront.

| Capability | Store | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant branding, CMS, catalog, pricing and stock | Available according to plan | Same foundation |
| Customer checkout, invoices, payments, delivery and aftercare | Tenant-owned seller group | One tenant invoice, potentially several seller orders |
| Staff roles, reporting and operational audit | Tenant staff only | Tenant staff plus separately authorized vendor workspaces |
| Vendor invitations and participation approval | New participation disabled | Allowed within entitlements and permissions |
| Vendor product submission and moderation | Disabled for new external offers | Allowed for approved sellers under review policy |
| Commission, settlement and vendor payouts | Not needed for new tenant-owned sales | Enabled where entitled; existing obligations always retained |
| Coupons, loyalty, wallets and gift cards | According to plan and eligibility | Same tenant-scoped rules; no cross-store value |
| Global discovery later | Optional, separate eligibility | Optional; every tenant/vendor offer still needs publication eligibility |

Mode, plan, business category and permission are separate concepts. A wholesaler can use either mode; paying for Marketplace does not approve a vendor, and a staff permission cannot unlock an unpurchased capability.

## One effective capability decision

An action requires the intersection of **tenant operational state, effective mode, plan entitlement, actor permission, record ownership/state and applicable approval**. The UI explains the decision; the server enforces it on API calls, imports, exports, scheduled jobs and integration commands. Feature flags may temporarily restrict rollout, but cannot grant commercial or security rights.

Existing `vendor_marketplace_enabled` and the settings registry must be audited before a new mode representation is selected. Prefer one canonical effective decision over two independent booleans that can disagree. Do not repurpose `TenantTypeEnum`; storage, compatibility and API changes need a separate design review.

## Store → Marketplace upgrade

1. An authorized tenant owner chooses the target plan/capabilities and sees the price, effective time and changed responsibilities.
2. Check the tenant's operational state, payment confirmation or approved trial, staff authority and version of commercial terms.
3. Prepare vendor policies: participation/review rules, commission basis, settlement schedule, payout responsibility and return/shipping ownership.
4. Persist the requested transition and billing evidence idempotently. Apply the mode/entitlement change atomically when prerequisites succeed.
5. Enable invitations and approvals. External offers remain unavailable until the tenant-vendor link, product publication, price and stock are eligible.

Existing orders, invoices, balances, URLs and tenant-owned products retain their identity and historical economics. Replaying the upgrade must not create duplicate charges or invitations. If provisioning fails after a payment, record a recoverable activation exception; do not silently leave the tenant charged but unusable.

## Marketplace → Store downgrade

Recommend an **obligation-aware, scheduled downgrade**, not an instant switch that hides vendor records.

| Stage | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Preview | Show target-plan limits, external offers, approved sellers, pending fulfillment, return/refund exposure and unsettled vendor money |
| Prepare | Merchant explicitly accepts closure steps; no new external seller approvals after the transition enters its closing stage |
| Stop new exposure | Disable new external-offer purchases and related discovery publication without rewriting existing purchases |
| Complete obligations | Preserve authorized fulfillment, returns, refund review, disputes, statements and payout processing until obligations are resolved |
| Finalize | Apply Store mode at the scheduled effective point only after the final checks pass |

Open financial/operational obligations block finalization by default. A still-open return window requires a servicing plan; it must not disappear merely because no return request exists yet. Any future exception allowing finalization with outstanding obligations needs a separately reviewed restricted-servicing workflow and explicit platform authorization. MVP does not provide that exception.

Do not delete vendors, historical products, seller order lines, documents or ledgers to satisfy a downgrade. Do not replace `vendor_id` with tenant ownership. Other approved workspaces of the global vendor remain unaffected. A cancelled downgrade restores only capabilities still allowed by the current paid plan and approvals.

## Existing tenant migration

Inventory current switch values and actual vendor relationships. Do not mass-classify stores from a default setting: an enabled flag may have been seeded without active external selling. Generate a proposed classification report, flag conflicts, obtain owner/platform confirmation and record the effective date. Never reprice existing purchases during migration.

## Required acceptance scenarios

- Direct API calls cannot create external offers in Store mode even if a hidden menu is bypassed.
- Upgrade preserves purchase history and creates no duplicate charge on retry.
- A vendor without approval cannot publish after the upgrade.
- Downgrade with a pending seller order or unpaid settlement is blocked with an actionable explanation.
- A global vendor's other tenant remains operational when one tenant downgrades.
- Staff without plan-management permission cannot change the mode; old mode values in a queued command are revalidated before creating new exposure.

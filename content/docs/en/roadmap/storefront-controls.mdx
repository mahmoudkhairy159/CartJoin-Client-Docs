## What a merchant should be able to do

Start with the shop as it looks today. Change its name, logos, colors, templates, navigation and translated content. Preview a mobile or desktop page in English or Arabic, publish it, and restore one setting or the entire design. Existing merchant content and historical invoices survive a design reset.

A merchant could include wishlist access for customers and allow exports for selected vendors. The platform administrator would see the package, the tenant setting, the person or vendor affected and the reason an action is available. Each merchant funds its own package. Vendor staff and buyers receive access in that store.

## Roadmap

| Stage | What becomes possible | Acceptance gate |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Capture defaults | Record the current design and available actions for each store mode and audience. | Existing stores retain their appearance, settings and access. |
| 2. Edit appearance | Change identity, theme and editorial sections in a private draft. Publish or reset with a preview. | Two stores show distinct identities on the same software, including browser titles, icons and mobile layouts. |
| 3. Define packages | Platform admins compare and publish versioned feature bundles and preview a merchant's effective rights. | Existing contracts retain their terms. A downgrade explains lost actions and when they take effect. |
| 4. Control audiences | Tenant and platform admins manage tenant features, vendor and customer defaults, and local exceptions. | A vendor or buyer can have different access at two stores. Direct actions follow the same rules as dashboard controls. |
| 5. Complete and release | Extend controls across templates and feature families. Pilot, verify and document each one. | Existing orders, refunds, balances and vendor obligations remain serviceable. Editors can trace and reverse changes. |

## How conflicting choices resolve

A package grants the merchant a maximum set of actions. The merchant can restrict them. If exports are available and the store permits exceptions, the merchant can enable exports for a single vendor. The vendor still needs an approved relationship and an authorized staff role. A store-wide or platform restriction takes precedence. Resetting the vendor selection returns it to the store default.

Customers keep their central CartJoin identity. Their access to a feature is specific to the shop. Guests see only the public experience. A feature change affecting new transactions must leave a usable route to service existing purchases.
